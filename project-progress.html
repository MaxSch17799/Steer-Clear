Project Progress

First MVP — summary of decisions, build, and validation

MVP Overview

Our first MVP is a retrofit collision-avoidance aid for ground support vehicles. It uses multiple ultrasonic sensors for distance measurement, a simple audio/visual UI for the operator, and a microcontroller-based architecture designed for low cost, modularity, and fast installation. Power is sourced from the vehicle’s 12–24 V system, with wiring kept simple and robust.

Key Design Decisions

1) Distance Sensing

We evaluated IR/ToF, lidar, radar, and mechanical contact. For MVP we chose ultrasonic (HC-SR04) for range (0–5 m), accuracy (~±1 cm), price, and simplicity. IR/ToF under-performed outdoors and on certain surfaces; lidar/radar were overkill for cost/complexity.

2) Redundancy & Filtering

We moved from 2 to 5 sensors to enable majority voting and reject outliers. This improved robustness and reduced false readings without complex filtering.

3) Operator Feedback

The airport environment is noisy and time-critical. We combined audio beeper + LEDs for quick attention, and added OLED + 20×4 LCD for detailed readouts and future expandability.

4) Electronics & Comms

Controller: Arduino Nano V3 (cheap, small, plenty of I/O). Data bus: I²C (fast to implement on Nano; CAN considered for later). Physical layer: wired copper for reliability and maintainability in the field.

5) Mounting & Modularity

We designed a flange-mount sensor housing with passive weather shielding (overhang + slanted roof) and chose robust GX16-4 connectors for plug-and-play. Position identity is set via DIP switches to avoid reflashing firmware on repositioning.

Build Snapshot

We started with a breadboard proof-of-concept, validated basic sensing + UI + I²C, then migrated to a modular harness with GX16-4 connectors and splash-resistant housings.

Validation Highlights

  • Detection zone: reliable detection in a 0–3 m danger zone suitable for docking speed profiles.
  • Responsiveness: sub-perceptual target (<200 ms) achieved in interactive use; majority voting reduces spurious spikes.
  • Modularity: hot-adding/removing sensors on the I²C bus supported; DIP-switch identity recognized on boot.
  • Operator UI: LEDs + beeper draw attention quickly; displays provide extra context when needed.

Next Steps

  • Field trials: validate on-vehicle at low speeds and near aircraft stand-ins.
  • CAN option: evaluate a CAN backbone for EMI-heavy environments and priority messaging.
  • Environmental hardening: elevate IP rating, consider heaters/wipers for snow/ice.
